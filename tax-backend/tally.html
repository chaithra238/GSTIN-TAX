What is GSTIN?

  • Every business registered under India's Goods and Services Tax framework is assigned a GSTIN - a 15-character alphanumeric identifier that acts as its tax identity. Unlike older tax systems that assign separate IDs for Excise, VAT, and Service Tax, GSTIN consolidates everything under one number, making compliance significantly simpler.
  • But here's the catch: not every GSTIN you receive from a vendor or buyer is legitimate. Fake or cancelled GSTINs are a real problem, and transacting with them can cost your business dearly through denied input tax credit, penalties, or GST notices.
  • That's why verifying a GSTIN before recording a transaction isn't just good practice — it's a compliance necessity.

Understanding GSTIN structure

Before verifying a GST number, it’s important to know its correct structure. A valid GSTIN is a 15-character alphanumeric code with the following format:

First 2 digits

  • Represent the state code of the registered person

Next 10 characters

  • Correspond to the PAN of the business or individual

13th digit

  • Indicates the entity number of the same PAN in a state (e.g., 1, 2, 3 for multiple registrations)

14th character

  • By default, the alphabet “Z”

15th digit

  • A check code (alphabet or number) used for error detection
  • If a GSTIN does not follow this format, it should be treated as invalid.

Why verify a GST number?

If you claim ITC based on an invoice from a supplier with a cancelled or fake GSTIN, the tax department can deny your credit — even if you paid the GST in good faith.

Ghost vendors and shell companies often use fabricated GSTINs on invoices. A quick verification takes seconds; recovering from a fraudulent transaction takes months.

An invalid GSTIN in your GSTR-1 data will trigger mismatches in your counterparty's GSTR-2B, leading to notices and reconciliation headaches.

A business that was once GST-registered may have had its registration cancelled — voluntarily or by the department. Their old GSTIN may still look valid at a glance, but searching it will show "Cancelled" status.

How to spot a fake GST vendor

The GSTIN returns no search results, indicating the number is entirely fabricated.
The legal name on the GST portal does not match the name printed on the invoice.
The vendor continues to issue invoices despite their GST registration being officially cancelled.
The registered business address is non-existent, residential, or occupied by an unrelated entity.
Commercial deals with "GST included" at impossible discounts often signal fraudulent ITC generation.
The vendor is reluctant or unusually slow to provide their GSTIN for verification.
The invoice lacks the mandatory, accurate HSN or SAC codes required for legal compliance.

Steps to urgent recovery from fake vendor

Follow these simple steps if you've accepted invoices from a fake or fraudulent GSTIN:

Step 1

What happens after you report?

Once a complaint is received, the GST department typically initiates a verification process. If the GSTIN is found to be fraudulent, the department can:

Suspend or cancel the registration immediately

Initiate a search and seizure operation at the business premises

Recover unpaid tax along with interest and penalties

Refer the case for prosecution under the GST Act, which can carry imprisonment of up to 5 years for cases involving tax evasion above ₹5 crore
